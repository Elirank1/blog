Something interesting is happening in AI. And I think we're looking at the beginning of an entirely new industry.

In May, Anthropic partnered with Blackstone, Hellman & Friedman, Goldman Sachs and others to establish what later became Ode: a company focused on deploying frontier AI into real business operations.

A week later, OpenAI announced its own Deployment Company, backed by major investment and consulting partners.

And just last week, the Financial Times reported that Anthropic and Hg are planning another AI deployment venture in Europe.

Three moves. Different structures. A remarkably similar direction.

---

The companies building the world's most advanced AI models are investing heavily in the people and organizations required to make those models useful inside businesses.

I think this deserves more attention than it's getting.

For the past few years, much of the AI conversation has focused on model capabilities, benchmarks, agents and automation. But deploying AI into an actual organization is a very different problem.

Someone still needs to understand how the business really operates. Not just its documented processes, but its unwritten rules, dependencies, exceptions, decision-making, economics and organizational constraints.

Someone needs to determine what should change, what shouldn't, what can realistically be built, and how to prove that the change creates value.

That work requires a rare combination of business judgment, engineering depth and organizational understanding. And it doesn't disappear just because the underlying models get smarter.

My interpretation of these moves is that AI deployment is becoming an industry in its own right.

The combination of frontier AI labs, private equity capital and specialized deployment teams is particularly interesting. The labs bring the technology. Private equity brings capital and access to operating companies. Deployment teams bridge the gap between technological capability and business outcomes.

That's a powerful model.

But it also raises what I believe is the more interesting question: if AI deployment becomes a massive industry, how will the deployment process itself scale?

Today, much of that process still depends on highly skilled people working closely with each organization. Hiring and training more experts is one way to scale. Building better tools for those experts is another.

But I suspect the next frontier goes deeper.

Can the expertise involved in understanding an organization, identifying valuable opportunities, evaluating constraints, designing solutions and validating outcomes become increasingly systematic, reusable and eventually software-defined?

Can knowledge gained from one deployment improve the next, without starting the entire discovery and engineering process over again?

And perhaps more importantly: can we rethink how organizations should operate in an AI-native world, rather than simply automate how they've always operated?

I don't think these are solved problems. And I certainly wouldn't underestimate how difficult they are.

But from what we're seeing in the market, the direction is becoming hard to ignore.

The first wave made intelligence accessible. The next wave is making intelligence operational. And I suspect the wave after that will focus on making the process of transformation itself scalable.

That's the part of this market I've become increasingly fascinated by.
