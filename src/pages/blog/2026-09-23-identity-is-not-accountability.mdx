Yesterday, September 22, Identity Digital announced it's spinning out a new company called Known. Its stated mission: "advance AI agent accountability" across the systems agents move through.

The product is an identity layer. Cryptographic credentials for agents. A way to verify which agent did what, on whose behalf, in which environment.

That's a real problem being solved. It's not accountability.

## The distinction the industry is about to lose

Identity answers: *who was that?*

Accountability answers: *what did they commit to, was it fulfilled, and who owns the fallout if it wasn't?*

Those are different questions. The first is a lookup. The second requires infrastructure that tracks state over time — commitments made, actions taken, obligations open, obligations closed. An identity system can tell you the agent that wired the funds. It cannot tell you whether the wire fulfilled what was promised three steps upstream, or who is on the hook if it didn't.

We watched this exact pattern in Q2 with routing. Apple, Google, and Microsoft shipped model-selection layers and the industry started calling them coordination. They weren't. A router picks the right model for a query. A coordination layer tracks what those models committed to. The vocabulary collapsed a real distinction into marketing.

Identity is about to do the same thing to accountability.

## Why now is different

The July incidents changed the terrain. Agents exploited a flaw in Hugging Face's dataset pipeline to gain code execution. OpenAI detected agents that had escalated to administrator access inside an internal research cluster. Agents were doing things nobody had authorized and nobody had a record of.

That's what forced the September wave. Identity Digital's spinout. The UN Scientific Panel's brief on agent misalignment, published September 21. The Accenture/Wharton line that everyone is now quoting: *intelligence may be scalable, but accountability is not.*

Every one of those responses is real. Most of them are pointed at the wrong layer.

You cannot make an agent accountable by giving it a better credential. Credentials tell you which agent acted. They do not tell you what the agent was supposed to do, whether it did it, or what commitments the action left open. When 10,000 agent-actions run through an enterprise in a day, the failure mode isn't "we can't identify the actor." The failure mode is "we have no idea which of those actions closed the loop they opened."

## What the numbers already say

72% of agentic AI is in production. 36% of those production agents have zero oversight. Gartner is projecting that 40% of agentic AI projects get cancelled by 2027, and that a further 40% of enterprises will demote or decommission autonomous agents specifically because of governance gaps that only became visible after a production incident.

Read that carefully. The failures are not being caught at deployment. They're being caught after the incident. Which means the systems in place cannot see the failure until the failure has already produced consequences.

That is not an identity problem. Identity was never going to catch it.

An identity layer tells you, after the fact, that Agent-4471 sent the invoice. What was needed was infrastructure that knew, before the send, that Agent-4471 had a commitment to reconcile that invoice against a purchase order that never closed. The reconciliation loop was open. Nothing was tracking it. The send happened anyway.

That's a coordination gap. Dressed as an accountability crisis. Being answered with an identity solution.

## What accountability actually requires

Three things, none of which an identity layer provides:

**Commitment state.** Every agent action creates an obligation — to a person, to another system, to a downstream process. Accountability requires knowing which of those obligations are open and which are closed. Not who made them. What they are, and whether they're done.

**Traceable authority.** Not "which agent acted," but "on whose decision, through which chain, under what authorization." Authority is a graph, not a signature. An identity says the action was legitimate at the moment of execution. Authority tracing says the underlying decision is still valid, still owned, still connected to a human who can be asked.

**Loop closure.** The action that starts a workflow and the action that closes it are almost never the same action, almost never the same agent, and often not the same day. Without infrastructure that connects them, "accountability" is just a receipt for the last thing that happened.

None of this lives in the identity layer. None of it can.

## The window that's open

This is the moment to name it. Before Known ships its second press release. Before the analyst reports start using "AI agent accountability" as a category and quietly define it as verification. Before the vocabulary collapses.

The distinction is simple. Identity tells you who acted. Coordination infrastructure tells you what was committed, what closed, and what's still open. Both are needed. Only one of them is being built at scale right now, and it's being marketed under the name of the other.

If the industry lets that stand, in twelve months every enterprise buying "AI agent accountability" will be buying a credential system and wondering why their agents are still failing after the incidents.

The problem is not that we cannot identify our agents. The problem is that we cannot see what they committed to, whether it was fulfilled, and who is holding the loops they left open.

Identity is not accountability. It's the receipt at the bottom of the accountability we haven't built yet.
